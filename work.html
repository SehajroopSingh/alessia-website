Analyse.

Researching how a system actually works, for the people trying to understand or change it.

Project 01 Analyse Socioeconomic Development · Behavioural Science

Understanding how policy works in practice

Dreamocracy · Policy Researcher and Analyst
June 2024 – July 2025
Brussels

A policy that looks perfect on paper still has to survive contact with actual people.

Context

Dreamocracy works on collective intelligence and public-interest innovation — the space between how policies are designed and how they actually land.

Question

What happens between a policy being designed and a person actually experiencing it?

What I did

Researched public-interest interventions and policy effectiveness — including behavioural drivers, emotions, incentives and institutional dynamics — working with perspectives from academia, government, business and civil society.

What emerged

A more granular understanding of why well-designed policies succeed or stall: rarely because of their formal logic, usually because of the incentives, emotions and institutional dynamics surrounding them. That research also fed directly into a workshop we then built and ran — Project 06.

What I learned

Policy effectiveness is a behavioural question as much as an institutional one. The most useful analysis holds both at once.

Project 02 Analyse AI · Socioeconomic Development

Understanding AI before trying to govern it

World AI Film Festival
Six months of cross-sector research
Nice

Governance debates conducted at a distance from practice tend to regulate an imagined technology.

Context

The audiovisual industries are among the first to feel AI's full disruptive force — creatively, economically and legally. The World AI Film Festival sits exactly at that fault line.

Question

What happens when technological change moves faster than the institutions designed to govern it?

What I did

Led six months of cross-sector qualitative research, interviewing experts across technology, cinema and the audiovisual industries on:

  • EU AI Act compliance
  • Copyright and intellectual property
  • Labour displacement in the creative industries
  • Misinformation, deepfakes and content authenticity
  • Platform responsibility and AI adoption

Including comparative analysis of AI legislation across the EU, the Americas and Asia.

What emerged

A cross-sector picture of how practitioners — not just regulators — actually understand, adopt and worry about AI, and where existing governance frameworks meet or miss their reality.

What I learned

Talking to the people building and using AI changes which questions seem urgent. Six months of interviews taught me more about where governance will succeed or fail than any framework document.

Translate.

Turning complex regulatory, data and policy requirements into something a specific audience can act on.

Project 03 Translate ESG & Sustainability

Designing better sustainability disclosure

Disal Consulting · Consultant · Client: Aquafil
August 2025 – Present
Rome

A disclosure is only as credible as the data architecture underneath it.

Context

Aquafil, a multinational fibre and plastics company, facing the EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive — a framework that asks organisations to translate complex regulatory requirements into reliable internal systems. One client, one underlying data problem: the report and the architecture beneath it are a single story.

Question

How do organisations turn complex sustainability requirements into systems people can actually use?

What I did

Authored a CSRD/ESRS-aligned sustainability disclosure report — ESG data collection and verification across Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions, gender pay gap and whistleblowing indicators, double materiality analysis, and audit-ready documentation. In parallel, worked on the underlying Tagetik data architecture: a custom CSRD-compliant configuration with a full audit trail, plus an EcoVadis value-chain analysis.

Evidence

The report was filed via ESEF ahead of deadline, built on a data architecture designed to make every figure traceable to its source.

What I learned

The hardest part of CSRD is not interpreting the standard — it is designing data collection that real people, in real departments, can sustain year after year.

Project 04 Translate Behavioural Science · ESG & Sustainability

Making regulation and change legible

Disal Consulting · Consultant
August 2025 – Present
Rome

Regulation only works when the people it affects can understand what it asks of them.

Context

Sustainability regulation moves fast, and the businesses, consumers and stakeholders it affects mostly experience it as noise. Someone has to turn regulatory and policy developments into something their audiences can follow and act on.

Question

How do you make regulatory and policy developments legible to the audiences who have to act on them?

What I did

Led B2B/B2C stakeholder communication and convening for a multinational client: an Instagram, blog and newsletter strategy; four webinars and eight podcast episodes with EU stakeholders on EPR and textiles, eco-design, and government and industry perspectives; and contributed to organising and delivering ABCD, a national cross-sector event.

Evidence

  • 42K Instagram followers, +7% engagement over 10 months
  • Webinar and podcast attendance up 40% year on year
  • ABCD: 1,398 tickets, 52% overall attendance, 86% paid-ticket conversion

What I learned

Communication is part of the system, not decoration on top of it. The same regulatory development, framed for the right audience at the right moment, produces engagement instead of noise.

Building a new system from scratch, tested against real users.

Project 05 Build AI · Behavioural Science · Socioeconomic Development

MemSurf

Co-Founder & Chief Operating Officer
January 2026 – Present
Los Angeles / Remote

The most honest test of ideas about how people learn is a product whose survival depends on them.

Context

MemSurf is an AI-native edtech startup: a learning app designed to make learning more accessible and effective through micro-learning, active recall and spaced repetition.

Question

Can we use AI not simply to automate learning, but to design around how humans actually think, learn and make decisions?

What I do

  • Adaptive quiz architecture and lesson structures
  • Product and content decisions, grounded in behavioural design
  • User interviews and product analytics with Amplitude
  • Product strategy and experimentation

Evidence

100+ users across Italy and the US — and a continuous stream of retention and behaviour data on how people actually learn, where they drop off, and which design decisions change behaviour.

What I'm learning

Building is the most honest test of ideas about behaviour and systems. Every assumption about how people learn gets confronted with analytics, interviews and retention data — quickly, and without mercy.

Project 06 Build Socioeconomic Development · Behavioural Science

Emotions in policymaking — the workshop

Dreamocracy · Co-moderator & designer
Brussels

Emotions are data about how a policy will actually land — so we built the tools to put them on the table.

Context

The research in Project 01 kept pointing to the same gap: policy discussions have no vocabulary for the emotional dynamics that decide how policies land. Rather than write another paper about it, we built a room where those dynamics could be worked with directly.

Question

Can emotional dynamics be made a working input to policy discussions — not an afterthought?

What I did

Co-moderated a policy workshop on emotions in policymaking, collaborating with academic and policy institutions — and designed the engagement tools and cards that structured the discussion, turning an abstract behavioural insight into something 70 people could physically work with.

Evidence

70 attendees from academia, government, business and civil society, working through the emotional dimensions of policy design with the tools we built — including the inspirational cards, which you can download and read.

What I learned

A tool in someone's hands does what a recommendation in a report cannot: it changes how the conversation itself runs. Building the format was the analysis, made usable.
