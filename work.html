Entrepreneurship · AI · Behaviour

MemSurf

Co-Founder & Chief Operating Officer
January 2026 – Present
Los Angeles / Remote

Context

MemSurf is an AI-native edtech startup: a learning app designed to make learning more accessible and effective through micro-learning, active recall and spaced repetition. It currently serves 100+ users across Italy and the US.

Question

Can we use AI not simply to automate learning, but to design around how humans actually think, learn and make decisions?

What I do

  • Adaptive quiz architecture and lesson structures
  • Product and content decisions, grounded in behavioural design
  • User interviews and product analytics with Amplitude
  • Product strategy and experimentation

What has emerged

A working product with real users in two markets — and a continuous stream of evidence about how people actually learn, where they drop off, and which design decisions change behaviour.

What I'm learning

Building is the most honest test of ideas about behaviour and systems. Every assumption about how people learn gets confronted with analytics, interviews and retention data — quickly, and without mercy.

ESG / CSRD

Designing better sustainability disclosure

Disal Consulting · Consultant
August 2025 – Present
Rome

Context

A multinational fibre and plastics client facing the EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive — a framework that asks organisations to translate complex regulatory requirements into reliable internal systems.

Question

How do organisations turn complex sustainability requirements into systems people can actually use?

What I did

Authored a CSRD/ESRS-aligned sustainability disclosure report — including ESG data collection and verification, double materiality analysis and audit-ready documentation.

What emerged

A disclosure report built to stand up to audit — and, underneath it, a clearer picture of how sustainability data actually moves through a multinational organisation.

What I learned

A reporting framework is only as good as the human system behind it. The hardest part of CSRD is not interpreting the standard — it is designing data collection that real people, in real departments, can sustain.

AI Governance

Understanding AI before trying to govern it

World AI Film Festival
Six months of research
Nice

Context

The audiovisual industries are among the first to feel AI's full disruptive force — creatively, economically and legally. The World AI Film Festival sits exactly at that fault line.

Question

What happens when technological change moves faster than the institutions designed to govern it?

What I did

Led six months of cross-sector qualitative research, interviewing experts across technology, cinema and the audiovisual industries on:

  • The EU AI Act
  • Copyright and intellectual property
  • Misinformation and deepfakes
  • Platform responsibility
  • AI adoption

What emerged

A cross-sector picture of how practitioners — not just regulators — actually understand, adopt and worry about AI, and where existing governance frameworks meet or miss their reality.

What I learned

Governance debates conducted at a distance from practice tend to regulate an imagined technology. Talking to the people building and using AI changes which questions seem urgent.

Policy / Behaviour

Dreamocracy — public-interest interventions

Policy Researcher and Analyst
June 2024 – July 2025
Brussels

Context

Dreamocracy works on collective intelligence and public-interest innovation — the space between how policies are designed and how they actually land.

Question

What happens between a policy being designed and a person actually experiencing it?

What I did

Researched public-interest interventions and policy effectiveness — including behavioural drivers, emotions, incentives and institutional dynamics — working with perspectives from academia, government, business and civil society.

What emerged

A more granular understanding of why well-designed policies succeed or stall: rarely because of their formal logic, usually because of the incentives, emotions and institutional dynamics surrounding them.

What I learned

Policy effectiveness is a behavioural question as much as an institutional one. The most useful analysis holds both at once.
