How do we design systems that work better for the people who have to navigate them?

My work moves between structural questions about how systems and institutions are designed and the individual question of how people experience and navigate them — in three ways: analysing how the systems actually work, translating what I find for the people who have to act on it, and occasionally building new systems from scratch.

01 Analyse.

How systems and institutions actually work, for the people trying to understand or change them.

02 Translate.

How complex regulation, data and requirements become something a specific audience can act on.

03 Build.

What changes when you stop studying a technology from the outside and start building with it.

Running through all three: ESG & Sustainability · AI · Socioeconomic Development · Behavioural Science — the four subjects these three actions are applied to, in different combinations on every project.

The veil we work through

We like to think of ourselves as objective observers of the systems around us. We aren't.

Our assumptions, incentives, experiences and cognitive shortcuts shape what we notice, what we ignore and how we interpret the world.

Some philosophical traditions describe this distance between reality and perception as Maya — the veil through which we experience the world.

Accounting for that veil does not make us less rational. It can make our institutions, policies, reporting systems and technologies more thoughtful.

This is why behavioural science runs through everything on this site. It is not a separate interest — it is the lens through which I approach systems: no policy, disclosure framework or technology is ever experienced directly. It is experienced through human cognition.
