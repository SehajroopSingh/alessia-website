Luiss

Workplace Biases: Decoding the Emotional Gender Divide and its Impact on Professional Dynamics

January 2024 · Advisor: Riccardo Viale

Why are women still assumed to be more emotional than men — and what does that cost them? How emotion stereotypes shape hiring, promotions and salary negotiations.

PDF
Abstract, question, methodology & findings
Abstract

This paper explores the pervasive stereotypes surrounding emotionality in gender dynamics, particularly within workplace settings. Despite the progress women have made in educational attainment and professional opportunities, gender biases persist, often manifesting in perceptions that women are inherently more emotional than men. This study critically analyzes the implications of these stereotypes for women in leadership positions, focusing on how they influence decision-making processes, hiring practices, promotions, and salary negotiations. Through a dual lens of statistical discrimination and gendered social norms, we examine the detrimental effects of emotionality stereotypes on women’s career advancement and organizational effectiveness. Additionally, we discuss the complexities of gender norms that shape emotional expressions and the biases that arise from them. By highlighting the limitations of existing research, particularly concerning diversity in study populations, the research advocates for a more inclusive approach to understanding gender-emotion dynamics and their broader implications for equality in the workplace.

Research question

To what extent do stereotypes about emotional differences between genders shape decisions in the workplace — in hiring, promotions and salary negotiations?

Methodology

A critical review of behavioural and economic research, read through two lenses — statistical discrimination and gendered social norms — including how anger, sadness and self-promotion are perceived depending on status and gender.

Key findings

Emotion stereotypes distort recruitment, pay and promotion, and leave women caught between competence and likeability. Real change means redefining the norms that govern behaviour, not only personal beliefs — for example by building debiased judgement procedures into organisational training.

The Uniqueness of the Bi-Regional Relationship Between the European Union and Latin America

2023 · Bachelor’s thesis, European Union law · Advisor: Robert Schütze

What sets the EU’s partnership with Latin America apart from the region’s ties with China or the United States — from its legal basis and Mercosur to sustainable development, trade and security.

Read article
Abstract, question, methodology & findings
Abstract

The European Union offers Latin America a wider range of external relations than China or the United States — from development cooperation and political dialogue to free trade agreements and coordination on global issues. This thesis examines the EU as a regional actor, the Latin American region and the concept of regionalism in international law: the origins and legal basis of the bi-regional partnership, its main instruments and agreements, and its evolution through Mercosur, foreign and security policy, sustainable development, and trade and investment.

Research question

What makes the bi-regional relationship between the European Union and Latin America unique — and what can each side offer the other?

Methodology

Legal and comparative analysis: regionalism in international law, the partnership’s legal basis, instruments and agreements, and comparative regionalism across foreign and security policy, sustainable development, and trade and investment.

Key findings

European cooperation increasingly reads as an offer of partnership for local development rather than poverty relief. Tensions remain — Latin American reindustrialisation against Europe’s need for raw materials such as lithium, and climate goals against the EU–Mercosur agreement — and to counterbalance China the EU must back shared principles with real financial incentives.

Luiss

Nudging Academic Responsibility: Investigating social media use among Apple users through behavioral insights

May 2023 · With S. Carrara, L. Lugli, F. Mondiello & F. Zampa

A behavioural intervention to curb excessive social media use among Luiss students — testing whether “rewarding” or “punishing” messages better nudge them towards Apple’s Screen Time controls.

Read article
Abstract, question, methodology & findings
Abstract

The pervasive use of social media among university students has raised significant concerns regarding its impact on academic performance and overall well-being. This paper investigates the excessive time spent on social media platforms, particularly among Luiss University students using Apple mobile devices, and proposes a behavioral intervention aimed at reducing this phenomenon. Utilizing the COM-B framework, which emphasizes the interplay of capability, opportunity, and motivation, we establish a baseline for social media usage through a short survey. The intervention employs targeted advertisements to enhance awareness of Apple’s screen time control features, encouraging students to prioritize their academic responsibilities over social media engagement. Our findings indicate a clear preference for “rewarding” advertisements over “punishing” ones, suggesting that positive reinforcement is more effective in promoting responsible media use.

Research question

How can more students be nudged to use Apple’s screen-time controls to limit the time they spend on social media?

Methodology

The COM-B framework (capability, opportunity, motivation); a short survey to set a baseline of social media use; and targeted advertisements raising awareness of Apple’s screen-time features, comparing “rewarding” and “punishing” framings.

Key findings

Students clearly preferred “rewarding” advertisements over “punishing” ones — positive reinforcement is the more effective nudge towards responsible media use.

Luiss

Exploring the Hijra community through colonial, racial, binary, and heteronormative discourses

May 2023

How colonial, racial, binary and heteronormative discourses marginalised the Hijra community in British-colonised India — read through feminist, postcolonial and intersectional lenses.

Read article
Abstract, question, methodology & findings
Abstract

This paper examines the intersection of colonial, racial, binary, and heteronormative discourses affecting the Hijra community in British-colonized India. Using feminist and postcolonial frameworks, it explores the marginalization and demonization of Hijras, analyzing how they have been portrayed as deviant within patriarchal and colonial narratives. The discussion employs Kimberlé Crenshaw’s intersectionality framework to dissect the multifaceted discrimination Hijras faced, considering structural, political, and representational intersections. Moreover, this study investigates the combined impact of the male and colonizer gazes, exemplified by British portrayals that framed Hijras as exoticized or dehumanized figures, reinforcing the colonial agenda of erasure.

Research question

How did colonial, racial, binary and heteronormative discourses shape the marginalisation of the Hijra community in British-colonised India?

Methodology

Feminist and postcolonial analysis using Kimberlé Crenshaw’s intersectionality framework (structural, political and representational), applied to literature, media and colonial policy such as the Criminal Tribes Act and Section 377.

Key findings

Colonial criminalisation curtailed Hijras’ social and economic roles, while British portrayals exoticised or dehumanised them. The community’s resilience challenges the binaries and hegemonic masculinities imposed by colonial powers — central to broader movements for identity reclamation in postcolonial societies.

From Classrooms to Boardrooms: The Intersection Between Gender, Education, and Behavioural Economics. A Comparative Analysis of China and India

2024 · Master’s thesis, Global Economic Governance & Public Affairs

Can education close the gender gaps that rapid growth left open in China and India? A comparative study across development and behavioural economics that proposes localised nudge units.

PDF
Abstract, question, methodology & findings
Abstract

This thesis examines the impact of education on improving women’s conditions in China and India, focusing on the disparity between the two countries’ rapid economic growth and the persistent gender inequalities in the labour market. Despite significant advancements in GDP, both nations continue to experience substantial gender discrimination in employment and wages. Operating within a multidisciplinary approach that combines development economics with behavioural economics, this research investigates the effectiveness of education as a tool to bridge gender gaps. The methodology includes a comparative analysis of educational policies and labour market trends in China and India, supported by both quantitative data and qualitative case studies.

Research question

Do educational and demographic interventions actually contribute to gender equality and sustainable development — or do they merely correlate with economic growth?

Methodology

A multidisciplinary, qualitative and comparative study of education policies for women and girls in China and India from the early 1970s to 2022 — drawing on reports, policy documents and data from the UN, UNESCO, the World Bank and the IMF, alongside case studies, with triangulation across sources.

Key findings

Education has improved women’s socio-economic status, but structural biases and social norms still hold back their progress and access to work. The thesis proposes localised nudge units to run behavioural interventions against gender bias in the labour market — a practical framework for making education policy work harder.

CIFE

Cross-country risk assessment: China & India

2024 · With L. Hellfors, M. Taplin, L. Vranica & R.K.R. Salvador

A social, economic and political risk assessment of China and India, written as recommendations to the UN Commission for Social Development on where support for decent work is needed most.

PDF
Abstract, question, methodology & findings
Abstract

Written for the UN Commission for Social Development’s priority theme, “full and productive employment and decent work for all”, the report analyses social, economic and political risk factors in the People’s Republic of China and the Republic of India, to recommend how aid should be distributed between the sectors facing the highest levels of risk.

Research question

Which sectors in China and India face the highest risk to full and productive employment and decent work — and where should aid go?

Methodology

A cross-country risk analysis in five parts — population health and happiness, political stability, current economic status, projected growth, and investment recommendations — drawing on indicators such as the World Happiness Report, the Human Development Index, unemployment, FDI and purchasing power parity.

Key findings

India faces especially high levels of risk: an HDI ranking of 134th, 6.8% unemployment and an increasingly unstable political environment. The report recommends investing in small business development, job training and primary education, through direct partnerships with regional governments and local non-profits.

Edinburgh

Technological ubiquity, social media and their effects on health: the importance of AI from robots to voice assistants

December 2022

How always-on technology shapes health — from humanoid robots in autism therapy and voice assistants in care, to social media’s risks for people with eating disorders.

Read article
Abstract, question, methodology & findings
Abstract

This essay explores the multifaceted impacts of technological ubiquity on individuals affected by autism and eating disorders, highlighting how digitalization shapes their experiences and healthcare interactions. Key drivers of technological ubiquity include rapid advancements in artificial intelligence, biotechnology, and increased accessibility to technology. The role of robotics, particularly in the form of interactive humanoid robots like NAO, illustrates positive applications in autism therapy by facilitating emotional recognition and interpersonal skills. The integration of voice assistants in healthcare settings exemplifies how technology can enhance patient support and streamline medical processes. However, the proliferation of digital media also poses risks for individuals with eating disorders, as social media can foster negative comparisons and unhealthy behaviors.

Research question

How does technological ubiquity — AI, robotics, voice assistants and social media — affect people living with autism and eating disorders, and the care they receive?

Methodology

An essay drawing on research into interactive humanoid robots (such as NAO) in autism therapy, voice assistants in healthcare settings, and the effects of social media on eating disorders — weighed against ethics, data privacy and bias in AI systems.

Key findings

Technology can support therapy and patient care, but digital media can also fuel harmful comparison for people with eating disorders. Its benefits depend on navigating ethics, data privacy and AI bias responsibly.
